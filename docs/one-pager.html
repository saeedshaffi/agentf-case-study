The migration experience

Product Design Engineer case · Saeed Shaffi · Nordlicht Werkzeuge GmbH, from first login to sign-off · npm start

The journey I designed

1ArrivalWhat loaded, in order, including the bank load that timed out and was retried. Rows read → duplicates → versions → 1,770 invoices. How sure we are.
2Your decisions168 questions in 9 groups, ordered by what they change: VAT first, cents last. Effect shown before booking; undo until sign-off.
3What we checkedEvery engine total recomputed from the records, plus 5 findings no question covers.
4BooksAny number opens: total → invoices → postings → source row → versions → bank payment.
5Hand-overChecklist, sign-off, read-only access for the advisor with a log of every answer.

Same data, three people: the finance lead gets plain words first; the tax advisor gets account numbers, definitions and the decision log, read-only, with contact data masked; the managing director gets one screen: did it work, does anything need me?

Decisions that mattered

  1. Order by consequence, not by count. A missing VAT ID on a customer we invoiced tax-free is not a one-cent rounding difference. Group stakes are fixed (VAT always first); wording, grouping and blocking are per-customer config.
  2. Read the data before designing the bulk action. For 10 of 11 supplier/description pairs the model suggested different accounts for identical inputs (88 of 89 invoices). So: "book all 37 to one account" with a preview, not "accept all suggestions".
  3. Every number is a button. One trace panel with a trail, in two registers: plain for the finance lead, accounting for the advisor.
  4. Recompute, and say where we disagree. This caught a real gap: the engine's €5,216,558.36 open receivables exclude 40 open credit notes (€560,940.33). Net: €4,655,618.03.
  5. Missing beats wrong. Each endpoint loads and fails on its own; no total is shown from partial data.
  6. A new customer is a folder, not a fork. customers/<id>/config.json: words, people, roles, groups, checks. npm run new-customer; tests validate every customer against its data.

What the screens surface that the engine did not ask

  • 4 overpayments (€4,365.62) matched as normal payments, no question raised
  • 11 tax-free EU sales (net €81,660.65) to a French customer whose VAT ID is "n/a"
  • 5 USD invoices with 19 % German VAT to a customer with no country
  • Open credit notes missing from open receivables
  • Inconsistent model suggestions

What I left out

  • Bank postings (not in the export, so not invented)
  • Real VIES checks and invitations (stubbed)
  • Server persistence and several users at once
  • Full translation and a WCAG audit
  • Mobile-first layout

How I would measure success

  • Faster: time and sessions from first login to sign-off (median and slowest quartile); drop-off per group.
  • Trust: trace opens per session falling over time; undo rate; migrations reopened after sign-off; advisor follow-up questions; corrections within 90 days.
  • Less help: migration support contacts per customer, by topic.
  • Guardrail: VAT corrections after the first return.

Built with an AI coding agent (log and transcript in the repository). Domain logic tested against the real data: 32 tests, money in integer cents, every engine total recomputed. Runs from a clean checkout with one command.
