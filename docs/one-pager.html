Decisions that mattered

  1. One workspace, three shapes of question. A focused view for consequential single questions (VAT ID, country), a side-by-side comparison for the duplicate customer, and evidence tables for the high-volume ones. Not one card type for 168 different problems.
  2. Never pre-select the machine's answer. I looked at the data before designing the bulk action. For 10 of 11 supplier-and-description pairs, the model suggested different accounts for identical inputs (88 of 89 invoices). So the product shows the spread and asks the person to decide once per description: "Book 37 invoices to 6825", with a preview and a one-step undo.
  3. Put the evidence where the decision is. For 20 of 24 one-cent questions, the bank shows the export's gross was paid, which contradicts the engine's suggestion. The table shows the payment next to the suggestion.
  4. Acknowledging is not fixing. Findings are Verified, Needs you, Acknowledged (still open) or Resolved by a named decision. "Ask the customer" on a required question keeps sign-off blocked, and the reason is named.
  5. Recompute, and say where the figures mislead. The engine's €5,216,558.36 open receivables leave out 40 open credit notes (€560,940.33). Net, customers owe €4,655,618.03, and that is the managing director's figure.
  6. A new customer is a folder, not a fork: a config with words, people, roles, question groups and checks, validated by tests against the customer's own data.

Found beyond the engine's questions

  • 4 invoices lowered after the customer had paid the original (€4,365.62 overpaid; no question raised)
  • Open receivables exclude open credit notes (above)
  • Inconsistent model suggestions (above)
  • 3 questions about USD amounts labelled EUR; shown in USD, never added to euro totals
  • The engine's question about VAT ID "n/a" is linked to the 10 invoices and 1 credit note booked tax-free (€81,660.65 net)

Left out, on purpose

  • Bank postings (matched, not posted, in the export; not invented)
  • Real VIES checks and e-mails: format check only, and the screens say so
  • A backend and real logins (the role switch is labelled as a prototype control)
  • German UI translation (account names stay German, with plain English beside them)

How I would measure success

  • Faster: median working time from first login to sign-off. Target: under 1 day, with no group open longer than 48 h.
  • Trust: under 5 % of migrations reopened after sign-off; fewer than 3 tax-advisor follow-up questions per migration.
  • Correctness: zero VAT corrections traced to migration answers in the first return.
  • Less help: migration support contacts per customer, by topic, falling release on release.

Built with AI coding agents and audited by independent agent reviews (log in the repository). 59 tests, every engine total recomputed in exact arithmetic, WCAG 2.2 AA checked with axe (0 violations on 12 screens) and by keyboard. Runs from a clean checkout with npm start.
